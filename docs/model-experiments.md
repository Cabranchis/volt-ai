# Model experiments

The tables are generated by `pipeline/research/report.py` from `pipeline/research/results/*.json`; the dated sections after them were written by hand.

## Protocol

- **Development origins** (selection only): every 30 days from 2020-03-01 to 2023-10-12, 45 origins; the first 8 only seed the online interval calibration, so 37 are scored. Every outcome precedes the first reported origin (2023-11-21).
- **Reported origins**: the 24 origins 45 days apart, 2023-11-21 to 2026-09-21 (the calendar of the model card until 2026-10-26, which now uses a different one: see its backtest protocol), plus the same 8 warm-up origins. Each finalist ran once on them (`results/reported_runs.json`); the harness refuses a second run.
- **Forecast date**: XM settles the price about three days late, so a forecast whose origin is the last settled day T is made on T + 3. Series XM publishes sooner (the programmed dispatch cost, hydrology) are read up to the day each is published by then, never beyond. A test scrambles every later value and requires identical features (`research/test_features_ext.py`, `tests/test_features.py`).
- **Metrics**: MASE and MAPE of the P50, mean pinball loss over P10, P50 and P90, coverage of the 80 % interval, and the paired per-origin MASE difference with a bootstrap 95 % interval (negative favours the candidate). Intervals of every candidate go through the same online conformal calibration.
- **Candidates tried**: 51 on the price and 7 on the demand, counting baselines and failures.

## Probability of a price spike

Events: price above 500 COP/kWh, above 800, and above 1.3 times its mean of the last 30 days, 1 to 14 days ahead. Brier score, lower is better. `Classifier` is a LightGBM binary model; `From quantiles` reads P(price > threshold) from the forecast's own calibrated P10, P50 and P90 as a log-normal (no new model); `ETS` does the same with the ETS interval; `Persistence` uses P(event | event today) and P(event | no event today) measured on the rows known at each origin; `Base rate` is the training frequency.

## Calibration that adapts to stress (2026-11-02)

Question: in stress the P10-P90 band covered 71 % (El Niño 2023-24) and 68 % (Sep-Nov 2024) of the days against the 80 % it promises. Does a calibration that adapts to the regime fix it? `research/run_calibration.py` re-calibrates the saved raw quantiles of `fs1_xgb` on the 37 scored development origins (nothing is retrained). V0 is the production calibration (every past origin, equal weight) and reproduces it exactly. The decision rule was written before the first run: a variant wins only if its coverage in the most volatile third of origins is at least 0.70 and closer to 0.80 than V0, its overall coverage is 0.80 +- 0.05, its mean relative width is at most 15 % above V0, and its mean pinball is not worse than V0.

| Variant | What it is | Coverage, all | Coverage, volatile third | Coverage, rest | Relative width | Pinball | Paired pinball vs V0 [95 %] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `V0` | current: every past origin, equal weight | 0.795 | 0.786 | 0.801 | 0.594 | 21.02 | |
| `V1` | recency weights, half-life of 6 origins | 0.820 | 0.832 | 0.814 | 0.712 | 22.03 | +1.01 [-0.62, +2.55] |
| `V2` | scores divided by the 28-day price volatility at their origin | 0.790 | 0.832 | 0.764 | 0.627 | 21.49 | +0.47 [-0.03, +0.82] |
| `V3` | only past origins of the same regime (stress or not) | 0.784 | 0.770 | 0.792 | 0.625 | 21.73 | +0.71 [+0.15, +1.15] |

Result: negative, no variant is shipped and the reported origins were not run. On the development origins the current calibration already covers 0.786 in the most volatile third, so there is nothing to fix there; V1 and V2 move that figure further from 0.80 (0.832), widen the band by 5 to 20 % and lose on pinball (V3 significantly). The reported origins were not used to look for a better variant.

Correction 2026-11-14: the "short band in stress" below (68 to 71 %) was measured on 4 to 8 dates per episode. On 151 dates 7 days apart the band covers 80 to 93 % in every period (see the section "Price backtest with test dates every 7 days"), so the premise of this experiment was weaker than stated; the negative result stands.

Why the band is short in 2023-24: the calibration is already doing most of the work (raw coverage 0.62 becomes 0.71 in El Niño 2023-24, and 0.38 becomes 0.68 in Sep-Nov 2024, production backtest, 50 origins). What is left is a regime break the centre of the forecast did not see (April 2024: price falls 90 % in two weeks; the ETS and seasonal naive references fail the same way). A wider band does not fix a wrong centre; new information does (rainfall, gas supply).

Exploratory finding, after the fact (not a validated predictor): on the 50 production origins, those made when the thermal share of generation (7 days) was above 30 % (11 origins) had MASE 3.65 and coverage 0.67, against 0.87 and 0.85 for the other 39. A reservoir level below 40 % (3 origins) and the combined stress flag (41 of 50 origins) are not informative, and neither is the 28-day volatility (MASE 1.52 against 1.38). The 11 origins come from two episodes (Sep 2023 to Apr 2024 and Sep to Nov 2024), so they are not independent; the threshold was not tuned but the four flags were looked at together. It becomes a candidate warning that must be confirmed on origins from the live record before it is shown as more than a note.

Against ETS and against repeating the last price the advantage is modest: over all dates the interval includes zero (-0.05 and -0.08), and it excludes zero only in calm periods (-0.13 and -0.16).
- In stress periods the model, ETS and repeating the last price are level; in El Niño 2026 both references are ahead on the averages (+0.19 and +0.23, intervals that include zero) at 4 to 14 days (8-14 days: 26.1 % against 20.2 % for ETS and 21.0 % for the last price). This matches the live record, where the model is no better than the last price at 4 to 7 days.
- The model's clear edge is at 1 to 3 days (12.0 % against 15.2 % for the last price) and in calm periods.
- The band covers 80 to 94 % in every period. The coverage of 68 to 71 % found on the 21-day dates in stress came from only 4 to 8 dates per episode; with the denser dates it is not reproduced, so the bands are not short in stress.
- The 8 to 14 day error is 36 % over all dates, and every method is above 35 % there.
- A first version of this script took the price of three days before the origin as "the last price" and made the reference look much weaker (26.6 % at 1-3 days); the origin day is the right one, because the origin is already the last settled day.

## Which methods can be told apart: Model Confidence Set (2026-11-15)

The paired intervals above compare the model with one reference at a time. The Model Confidence Set (Hansen, Lunde and Nason 2011, range statistic, `research/mcs.py`, `research/run_mcs.py`) asks the question for all the methods at once: which of them cannot be distinguished from the best one at the 10 % level, with the multiple comparisons inside each row accounted for. It uses the MASE of each of the 151 dense dates, a circular block bootstrap of 6 dates (consecutive dates share outcomes; the block of the paired intervals) and 5 000 resamples. The decision rule was written before the first run: the production model is supported if it is in the set for the whole horizon. Verdict: supported, and not distinguishable from ETS or from repeating the last price. It only reads the saved dense backtest; the 51 candidates of the tables above keep only averages, so they cannot be put in a set without running them again.

| Horizon | Model | ETS | Last price | Weekly seasonal naive | In the set |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All (1-14 days) | 1.520 | 1.572 | 1.601 | 2.045 | model, ETS, last price |
| 1-3 days | 0.707 | 0.844 | 0.849 | 1.676 | model |
| 4-7 days | 1.292 | 1.410 | 1.459 | 1.767 | model |
| 8-14 days | 2.000 | 1.977 | 2.004 | 2.361 | model, ETS, last price |

Mean MASE per method. The MCS p-value of a method is the smallest level at which it would still be in the set (it is not a test of the model against that method). ETS and the last price have 0.008 and 0.008 at 1-3 days, and 0.083 and 0.036 at 4-7 days; over the whole horizon they have 0.52 and 0.43, and at 8-14 days 1.00 (ETS, the best there) and 0.57.

What it says:
- At 1 to 3 days the model is the only method in the set, so it is better than every reference at once. At 4 to 7 days it is alone only at the 10 % level: at 5 % ETS stays in the set (p = 0.083).
- Over the whole horizon and at 8 to 14 days the model, ETS and repeating the last price cannot be told apart. This agrees with the paired intervals above.
- The 4 to 7 day result is pooled over 2023-26 and does not hold in every period. The live record has the model no better than the last price at 4 to 7 days, and in El Niño 2026 the table above has the model at 16.2 % against 13.7 % for ETS and 14.1 % for the last price. It does not support a claim for that range; the claim the evidence supports is better than the references at 1 to 3 days, and not distinguishable from them beyond.
- The weekly seasonal naive is out of the set in every row.
- A set that contains a method does not say that the method is as good; with 151 dates it says the data cannot separate them. Each row is a separate set: the control of multiple comparisons does not extend from one row to another.

## Does a correlation that fades with distance improve the cost range? (2026-11-15)

The cost calculator gives every pair of days in a window the same correlation, 0.7 (chosen on the 51 production dates). The errors of two days are in fact less alike the further apart they are. `research/run_window_correlation.py` measures it on the 151 dense dates, with the error of each day read against its own calibrated band, and asks whether using the real structure gives a better range for the mean price of a window. The structure is fitted on the first 75 dates and judged on the last 76, never on the dates it was fitted on. The decision rule was written before the run: a new structure replaces 0.7 only if its coverage is closer to 0.80 on average over windows of 3, 7 and 12 days and its pinball loss is not larger at any length.

Correlation of the standardised errors of two days a given distance apart (first half / second half of the dates):

| Days apart | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correlation | 0.80 / 0.81 | 0.70 / 0.70 | 0.64 / 0.63 | 0.59 / 0.56 | 0.54 / 0.50 | 0.51 / 0.48 | 0.48 / 0.44 |

The fade is real and the same in both halves. Fitted on the first half: AR(1) with phi = 0.885, and a common factor plus AR(1) with 0.227 + 0.773 * 0.821^lag. Results on the second half (coverage of the P10-P90 range of the window mean, share of windows below P10, mean pinball loss):

| Window | Equal 0.7 | AR(1) | Factor + AR(1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 days | 0.895 / 0.092 / 12.57 | 0.921 / 0.079 / 12.76 | 0.921 / 0.079 / 12.73 |
| 7 days | 0.868 / 0.092 / 17.54 | 0.868 / 0.092 / 17.62 | 0.868 / 0.092 / 17.58 |
| 12 days | 0.855 / 0.118 / 21.75 | 0.855 / 0.118 / 21.67 | 0.842 / 0.132 / 21.66 |

What it says:
- Neither structure replaces 0.7. Their distance of the coverage from 0.80 is no smaller (0.081 and 0.077 against 0.073) and the pinball is larger at 3 and 7 days. The calculator does not change.
- The structure matters little because the windows start at the third day of the forecast and the errors of nearby days are already strongly correlated (0.7 to 0.8): the single number 0.7 is close to the average correlation inside a window of 3 to 7 days. The fade only shows in the 12-day window, where the difference in loss is 0.1 on a loss of 21.7.
- The range of a window covers 84 to 92 % on these dates, wider than the 80 % it promises, and the misses fall mostly below P10 (8 to 13 %) rather than above (1 to 4 %). What would improve the range is a better centre or a narrower band for each day, not a different correlation.

## The reservoir water balance as a differential equation (2026-11-15)

Question: does the price depend on where the national reservoir will stand, not only where it stands now? The reservoir obeys dV/dt = I(t) - R, with I the inflows and R the net outflow (generation, spill, evaporation). `features_ext.hydro_ode_projection` solves it forward for each target day: R is the mean of I - dV over the last 28 days, the inflow anomaly l = log(I / historical mean) relaxes as dl/dt = -l / tau, l(t) = l0 exp(-t / tau), with tau from the autocorrelation of the anomaly up to that day, and the volume is clipped to [0, capacity] at every step. Two features per horizon: the projected share of the capacity on the target day and its change from today. Horizons 1 and 2 add nothing (the newest published level is already two days past the origin, and it is a feature of the production model); the projection starts at horizon 3. A test compares the code with the numerical solution of the equation on a synthetic case, and the leakage test scrambles every value after its publication day (and one day before, to prove the test can see it).

Two measurements on the days before the first reported origin (`research/run_hydro_ode.py`):
- The balance closes: over 1 785 days from 2019, the 28-day means of I - dV and of generation plus spill correlate 0.986 and differ by 2 % (ratio 1.02).
- The projection does not forecast the level better than leaving it where it is. Mean absolute error of the share of the capacity on the 45 development origins, projection against "the level stays": 0.0030 against 0.0028 at 3 days, 0.0131 against 0.0120 at 7 days, 0.0318 against 0.0258 at 14 days.

Experiment `fb_hydro_ode`: the production features plus the two, same LightGBM, on the 37 scored development origins. The decision rule was written before the run: candidate for production only if the interval of the paired per-origin MASE difference against `dispatch+contracts+hydro` is entirely below zero, the pinball is not larger and the coverage is between 0.75 and 0.85.

| | `dispatch+contracts+hydro` | + water balance | Difference (new minus reference) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MASE, all horizons | 1.504 | 1.464 | -0.040 [-0.112, +0.022] |
| MASE, 1-3 days | 0.529 | 0.498 | |
| MASE, 4-7 days | 1.278 | 1.291 | |
| MASE, 8-14 days | 2.051 | 1.978 | |
| Mean pinball | 21.21 | 20.76 | -0.45 [-1.07, +0.09] |
| P10-P90 coverage | 0.79 | 0.79 | |

The new model is ahead on 20 of 37 origins in MASE and 22 in pinball (Wilcoxon p = 0.52 and 0.40).

What it says:
- The rule fails: the interval of the MASE difference includes zero, so the block is one more candidate (51 on the price) and the production model does not change. The reported origins were not run.
- The sign is favourable (-0.040 MASE, -0.45 pinball) and the second-round blocks were all on the other side (+0.01 to +0.04, intervals that include zero), but 37 origins cannot tell a gain of this size from noise. The 1 to 3 day difference (-0.031) cannot come from the projection, which starts at horizon 3: it shows how much the result moves just from adding two columns to a model that samples features and rows at random.
- The balance holds as an accounting identity, but its solution does not predict the level better than persistence, because the net outflow and the inflows are not constant over two weeks. A feature that forecasts the level worse than the level itself cannot be expected to help the price, which agrees with the result.

## Why the live demand range covers less than 80 % (2026-11-19)

The Models page shows the live record: of the demand forecasts of 4 to 30 days, 62 to 70 % fell inside the P10-P90 range, against the 80 % it promises and 78 to 84 % in the backtest. `research/run_live_demand.py` reads the live record (forecast_log as first issued, from the weekly backup of 2026-11-15) and the real demand, and groups the forecasts by the week of the day they forecast. It is a diagnosis; no decision rule and no change to the model.

Share of forecasts inside the range, by week of the target day (345 forecasts from 23 origins, 26 target days):

| Target days | Forecasts | Inside P10-P90 | Above P90 | Mean error of the P50 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 22-28 Oct | 28 | 100 % | 0 % | +0.3 % |
| 29 Oct-4 Nov | 77 | 99 % | 0 % | +0.2 % |
| 5-11 Nov | 126 | 69 % | 25 % | -0.5 % |
| 12-16 Nov | 114 | 45 % | 55 % | -3.2 % |

What it says:
- The misses are concentrated in the last two weeks and all fall above P90: demand came in 3 to 5 % above the P50 on most days from 10 November (Tuesday to Friday of that week at 259 to 264 GWh against a P50 near 251). In the first two weeks the range covered 99 to 100 %, wider than needed. One event moved every horizon at once, so the 345 forecasts carry the evidence of one episode, not of 345.
- The horizon pattern of the Models page is partly an artefact of when the record starts. It began on 21 October, so the 15 to 30 day forecasts that can be scored are all for days after 5 November, inside the unusual weeks (all 78 of them; in the first two weeks only horizons up to 14 days exist). Within the same week the coverage does not fall steadily with the horizon (week of 5 Nov: 71 %, 54 %, 74 %, 75 % for 1-3, 4-7, 8-14, 15-30 days).
- The days coincide with a dry and slightly warmer spell (national rain 0.0 to 2.8 mm a day from 9 November against 1.5 to 12.4 mm before, mean temperature 22.7 to 22.9 °C against 21.1 to 22.5 °C). The coincidence is not shown to be the cause.
- The backtest does not point the same way: on 51 dates the misses at 1 to 7 days fall mostly below P10 (15 % against 7 % above), the opposite of the live direction. There is no consistent bias to correct.
- Conclusion: the evidence does not justify recalibrating the demand range. The honest reading of the Models page is "one atypical week moved the live figures"; it should be read again with more weeks. A demand surprise of this kind is what a weather forecast as an input could anticipate: the rain and temperature forecasts are archived (`weather_forecast_log`) and their evaluation is scheduled for 15 February 2027.
